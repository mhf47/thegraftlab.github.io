Development - Signaling - Regeneration

Investigating How Plants Connect, Communicate, and Regenerate

We investigate how plants communicate across cells, tissues, and organs to coordinate development, regeneration, and responses to their environment. By uncovering the mechanisms underlying these processes, we aim to understand how plants function as integrated systems and harness their signaling networks to engineer new traits and improve crop performance.

What we study

Decoding and Recoding Plant Communication

From cellular regeneration to long-distance signaling and programmable plant systems.

Cellular Communication and the Biology of Regeneration

We use compatible and incompatible graft combinations to investigate the role of intercellular communication in directing regenerative trajectories. While compatible grafts coordinate regenerative repair, reconnecting the vascular system, incompatible grafts fail to repair, and instead, undergo de novo organogenesis.

Long-Distance Signaling and the Molecular Language of Plants

We investigate how plants communicate across long distances, from the movement of RNAs and other molecular signals to the propagation of rapid signals through plant tissues. By combining experimental and theoretical approaches, we seek to understand how information is transmitted, interpreted, and integrated across the plant.

Programming Communication

Plants rely on long-distance signaling networks to coordinate growth and respond to their environment. As part of the NSF Center for Research on Programmable Plant Systems (CROPPS), we are investigating how these native communication pathways can be rewired to create programmable plants. Our research focuses on reengineering peptide signaling networks to connect environmental sensing with developmental responses, laying the groundwork for crops that can more efficiently use water and nutrients.

Resilient rootstocks

We are testing the role of rootstocks in enhancing vegetable crop productivity and stress resilience. This work combines experiments with commercial rootstocks and new rootstocks generated from tomato wild relatives.
